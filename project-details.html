Project of the Month

DIY Watercolor Landscape Painting

Create a stunning watercolor landscape with this beginner-friendly project. Learn essential watercolor techniques including wet-on-wet, layering, and color blending while painting a beautiful sunset landscape. Perfect for artists of all skill levels looking to explore the magic of watercolors.

Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 2-3 hours
Category: Painting
Shop Materials Needed Back to Home

Materials Needed

Everything required to complete this watercolor landscape project from start to finish.

Watercolor paper (300gsm)
Heavyweight cold-pressed paper to prevent buckling
Watercolor paints (12-color set)
Vibrant artist-grade pigments (cadmium yellow, orange, ultramarine, indigo)
Masking tape
Acid-free low-tack tape for crisp border lines
2 brushes (round & flat)
Size 8 round brush for blending & 1/2" flat wash brush
Pencil
2B pencil for soft horizon and contour guidelines

Step-by-Step Tutorial

1

Paper Preparation & Taping

Secure your 300gsm cold-pressed watercolor paper to a sturdy flat board using low-tack painter's tape along all four edges. Press firmly along the inner edge to ensure clean, razor-sharp borders and stop the paper from warping when wet.

2

Light Horizon Sketch

With a light 2B pencil, gently sketch a horizon line in the lower third of the paper. Add loose outlines for distant mountain ridges across the midground. Keep pencil marks very light so they don't show through transparent watercolor glazes.

3

Wet-on-Wet Sunset Sky Wash

Evenly wet the sky section with clean water using your flat brush. Begin applying warm lemon yellow near the horizon, blending smoothly upward into glowing cadmium orange, rose madder, and deep cobalt violet at the top of the sheet.

4

Atmospheric Mountain Silhouettes

Once the sky wash dries completely, glaze translucent lavender on the furthest ridge. Mix a richer indigo and Payne's gray for the nearer hills to produce realistic atmospheric depth and layered perspective.

5

Water Surface & Foreground Details

Paint mirror reflections on the foreground lake with light horizontal strokes reflecting the sky tones. Use the tip of your round brush to add crisp pine tree silhouettes and grassy shoreline banks.

6

Final Drying & Border Reveal

Let your painting dry naturally. Slowly peel away the masking tape at a 45-degree angle away from the paper to reveal your clean, framed watercolor artwork.

Artist Pro Tips

Two Water Jars: Keep two jars of water at your station: one for rinsing dirty pigments, and one with clean water for wetting the paper.
Gravity Angle: Work at a slight tilt (around 15 degrees) so gravity pulls the watercolor wash smoothly downwards without pooling.
Test Swatches: Test your color mixture and transparency on a scrap piece of watercolor paper before touching your main piece.
